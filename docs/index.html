Sector Pulse: where the jobs are and where the money went

Five years of payroll jobs, job postings and stock-market returns by sector, for California and the whole United States, plus a hand-collected look at the last three months, at which occupations and college majors the numbers favor, and at a one-day snapshot of about 39,000 live job postings. Which industries are hiring, which are shrinking, which ones investors rewarded, and what that means for someone looking for an entry-level analyst role.

    Data. Payroll jobs: U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, Current Employment Statistics (national, state and metro), via FRED. Stock sectors: month-end adjusted prices of the SPDR Select Sector funds and SPY, from Yahoo Finance. Last three months: BLS JOLTS and the Challenger, Gray & Christmas job-cut reports, typed in by hand and checked against the published totals. Careers and majors: BLS Employment Projections 2025–35 and the Federal Reserve Bank of New York's outcomes by college major. Live postings: public job APIs of Greenhouse, Ashby, Lever and SmartRecruiters (the systems behind employers' careers pages) and the remote job boards Remotive, Remote OK and Jobicy, collected on one day. Job postings: Indeed Hiring Lab job postings index (seasonally adjusted, 7-day average, Feb 1 2020 = 100). An index measures the trend in postings, not the number of open jobs.

    Method. Python (pandas) cleans the files and loads them into SQLite; the analysis is written in SQL (CTEs, window functions). Code, queries and results: github.com/resulisik/california-jobs-by-sector.
